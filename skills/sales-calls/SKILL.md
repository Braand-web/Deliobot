# Sales calls

Selling is asking the right questions and letting the prospect hear their own answers. Act like a doctor: they came
with a problem; you ask about the symptoms, say the diagnosis back better than they can, then prescribe.

For a service of real value, use three calls rather than forcing one.

## Call 1: the interview

Open by naming the frame: "Thanks for coming. Can I give you some context? I do this to learn from people like you, and
also because I help businesses like yours, so if I see a way to help, we can talk about it at the end. Fair enough?"
At the end, ask for a "no" rather than a "yes": "Would it be a crazy idea to book a call where I show you how we could
fix <problem>?" A question that can be answered with "no" feels safe.

## Call 2: the gap (never pitch here)

Find four things: **where they are now**, **where they want to be**, **what stops them**, **why now**. Then let them do
the maths aloud:

- "If I brought you 10 qualified leads a month, how many would you close?" (3)
- "What is a new client worth to you?" (500 000)
- "So what is not fixing this costing you each month?" ... silence ... (1 500 000) "And over a year?"

## Call 3: the growth plan

Play back their words and their numbers, then the price beside what the problem costs them: the return is obvious.
Have the payment link ready (Wave, Orange Money, Stripe…); ask for the decision on the call, without pressure.

## Objections

People first blame circumstances (time, money, fit), then other people (my partner, the economy), and only last
themselves. Ask what is really behind it, answer that, and come back to their own numbers. Never leave a call without
the next call booked.

## How you help

- **Before**: a one-page prep sheet (what is known about them, the gap questions, likely objections, the offer).
- **After**: when the person has a recording and the other party agreed to it being recorded, transcribe it with
  `transcribe_audio`, then score the call: share of talking time, gap found and quantified, next step booked, objections
  and how they were handled, and three things to do better. Draft the proposal and the follow-up e-mail for the person
  to send.
- Never invent a case study or a figure, and never pressure or mislead the prospect.
